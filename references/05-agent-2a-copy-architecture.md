# 05 — AGENT 2A: COPY ARCHITECTURE

The copy is assembled from the house's real modules, inside the claims envelope, using only
sourced facts. Target length for the body: **3,000–4,500 words**.

---

## 1. The module order that works

```
 H1        keyword-forward promise (not a pun, not a question-only)
 LEAD      2 paragraphs: the operational problem, then "By the end you will know…"
 QUICK     4–6 bullets, every bullet carries a number
 TOC       "On this page" jump links
 ANSWER    the definition paragraph an LLM would quote verbatim
 STATS     3–4 value+label cards
 WHY NOW   seasonal/operational trigger (the calendar hook lands here)
 FRAME     named framework, H3 per step
 LIST      numbered value list ("5 ways…", "6 areas…")
 COST      cost-per-use block  ← the house signature
 CHOOSE    format/product chooser + comparison table
 SELF      self-assessment / calculator / score
 CHECK     readiness checklist bound to the calendar deadline
 FAQ       6–10 long-form Q&As
 READING   3–4 internal blog links ("Keep Reading")
 CLOSE     closing argument + 2–4 arrow CTAs
```

Skip a module only for a reason you can state. A post without QUICK, FAQ and CHECK is off-pattern.

---

## 2. The cost-per-use block (the house's strongest device)

Structure it as arithmetic the reader can redo for their own operation. Three moves:

1. **Our price, per use.** `$36.99 ÷ 400 wipes ≈ $0.09 per wipe.`
2. **Their consumption.** Give a formula, not a claim —
   `tables × turns per day × wipes per reset × days open`.
3. **The real cost of the current setup.** Restock trips, running out mid-service, using more
   than needed. Use a cited third-party figure (e.g. paper towels ≈5¢/sheet) or the house's
   own published framing. **Never** a cleaning-performance comparison.

Compliance guard: no percentage, no "outperforms", no "safe" may sit inside this block.
Cost and format comparisons only.

---

## 3. Writing rules

- **Numbers or nothing.** "Cuts through residue" ✔ · "much better at cleaning" ✘
- **Paragraph discipline.** 35–55 words average; never above ~120 except in FAQ answers.
- **Active voice.** Target <10% passive.
- **Readability.** Flesch-Kincaid 6–8. Sentences <15 words average.
- **Banned AI-isms:** "delve into", "in today's world", "let's explore", "unlock",
  "revolutionize", "game-changer", "in the realm of", "it's important to note".
- **Direct address.** "Your floor", "your bussers", "your reset time" — write to one operator.
- **Claim tagging.** Mark each claim-bearing sentence with its code, e.g.
  `[N]`, `[S3]`, `[C]`, `[Eco]`, `[NSF/A-004]`, `[T3]`. Untagged claim-bearing sentences are bugs.
- **Patch-test line** after any material list.
- **Compliance block** for health/illness topics.
- **No fabricated proof.** No invented testimonials, no invented "200+ studios", no invented
  test numbers. If the house has real proof, use it; otherwise omit the proof, not the honesty.

---

## 4. CTA ladder (four rungs)

| Rung | Placement | Character | Example shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | after QUICK/WHY NOW | soft, informational | "See the cost per table" |
| 2 | after COST/CHOOSE | consideration, product-led | "Compare Autumn vs Unscented" |
| 3 | after FAQ | action, friction-reducing | "Print the food-service checklist" |
| 4 | CLOSE | the brief's own CTA | "Reduce Table Turnover Time" |

Anchor style in the house: verb + product/collection + arrow.
`Shop Table Bussers® Autumn-Scented →` · `Equip Your Service Stations →`

## 5. Worked skeleton (test blog, food-service, cost + labour + speed)

```markdown
# How to Reduce Restaurant Table Turnover Time: A Cost-per-Use Guide for Food Service

[LEAD] Every minute a table sits dirty is revenue you already paid labour for.
[LEAD] By the end you will know your cost per table reset and where the minutes go.

[QUICK] • reset target: under 90 seconds • casual dining averages 1.5 turns/hr
        • 0.3 extra turns on 60 seats ≈ $275,000/yr • our bucket ≈ $0.09/wipe
[TOC]
[ANSWER] Table turnover time is the elapsed time between a party leaving and the table
         being ready — its slowest step is usually the reset.
[STATS] 90 sec | reset target · 1.5/hr | turns · 30–35% | labour share of costs
[WHY NOW] Q4: year-end planning, budgets and par levels
[FRAME] The four-step reset: Clear → Clean → Reset → Seat
[LIST]  5 places minutes hide in a reset
[COST]  $/wipe · consumption formula · cost of the current setup
[CHOOSE] Autumn (seasonal, NSF-certified) vs Unscented (fragrance-free) + comparison table
[SELF]  Reset-time and par-level self-assessment
[CHECK] Q4 front-of-house readiness checklist
[FAQ]   6–10 questions
[READING] link the 2026-08-31 busser-efficiency post + 2–3 cluster posts
[CLOSE] Reduce Table Turnover Time → product + collection links
```

Note how the August post's territory (the four-step workflow) is *referenced and linked*,
not rewritten. That is how cannibalisation is avoided.
